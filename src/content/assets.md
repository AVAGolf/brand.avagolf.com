      - >-
        Sculptural forms shot in high-gloss black, folding into the angle of the
        AVA mark with a ball held in the negative space. The lighting does the
        work here — highlights trace the edges of the shape while the rest falls
        away into near-black, so the image reads as depth rather than detail.
      - >-
        This is a background layer, not a standalone image. It is designed to sit
        full-bleed behind the green overlay, which pulls the black toward the
        brand palette and drops the contrast far enough for type to sit on top.
        Used raw, without the overlay, it is too high-contrast and will fight
        anything placed over it.
      - >-
        Give the apex of the form room to breathe. Crop so headlines clear the
        point where the ribbons converge, and let the darker lower half carry
        body copy or navigation.

  - title: Green Overlay
    alt: AVA Golf green overlay — wave gradient texture
    image: texture
    caption: Wave / Gradient
    body:
      - >-
        This texture and treatment can be used as a background to create depth
        and personality to a page or piece of collateral.
      - >-
        Lay it full-bleed over background imagery, or use it on its own behind
        hero sections, section breaks and covers. Scale it so the wave sweeps
        across the composition rather than sitting centered in it, and let the
        gradient run off at least two edges — it should feel like a crop of
        something larger, never a contained shape.
      - >-
        Keep type on the darker side of the sweep and let the brighter band fall
        into open space. Crop rather than stretch, so the grain stays at its
        native scale; distorting it flattens the texture and makes the noise read
        as compression artifacts.

  - title: Halftones
    alt: AVA Golf brand halftone pattern
    image: halftones
    caption: Halftone dot pattern
    body:
      - >-
        Halftoning can be used to create an extra layer of graphical detail. This
        can be used over the texture or on top of elements such as photography
        etc. Ideally these should be kept to the primary color palette.
      - >-
        It works hardest at the edges of a composition — anchoring a corner,
        running along a boundary, or fading across a panel — where it adds a
        printed quality without introducing another color. Let the dots dissolve
        rather than stop; a hard edge on a halftone reads as a mistake.
      - >-
        Keep it clear of body copy, and step the density down before it crosses
        anything that has to stay legible. Over photography, drop the opacity far
        enough that the pattern reads as texture rather than a screen laid on top.
